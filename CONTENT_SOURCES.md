# Content verification notes

The cultural profile copy was checked against government tourism / Incredible India material and, for the UNESCO note, UNESCO's own Intangible Cultural Heritage records.

Primary references used during drafting:
- Karnataka Tourism / Incredible India: Mysuru Dasara, Yakshagana, silk and regional arts.
- Kerala Tourism: traditional cultural forms and festival pages for Kathakali, Mohiniyattam, Theyyam, Onam and Thrissur Pooram.
- Tamil Nadu / Incredible India: Chennai and Tamil Nadu cultural, Bharatanatyam, Pongal and cuisine references.
- Maharashtra Tourism: Ganesh Chaturthi and regional cultural material.
- Rajasthan Tourism: state culture, dance, music, crafts and cuisine.
- West Bengal Tourism: Durga Puja and cultural tourism.
- Gujarat Tourism / Incredible India: Navratri, Garba and Dandiya traditions.
- Uttar Pradesh / Incredible India: regional culture, food, crafts and heritage references.
- Punjab / Incredible India: Bhangra, Giddha, Phulkari, festivals and cuisine.
- Assam / Incredible India: Bihu, Sattriya, Mekhela Chador and Muga silk.
- UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage: “Durga Puja in Kolkata” (inscribed 2021) and the India country list.

Conservative wording was deliberately used for folklore and some dress/cuisine descriptions to avoid presenting community-specific variations as universal facts.

## Heritage at Risk sources

The new Heritage at Risk section uses source-aware language and does not treat a category label as an official national risk classification unless the cited source supports it. Key references include:

- Development Commissioner (Handicrafts), Ministry of Textiles: Crafts of India and the official list of crafts identified as endangered.
- UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage: India listing and the oral-traditions safeguarding framework.
- Sangeet Natak Akademi, Ministry of Culture: Intangible Cultural Heritage and Folk & Tribal safeguarding programmes.
- Press Information Bureau, Ministry of Culture: documentation and preservation of endangered folk languages and oral traditions.

The UI distinguishes "At Risk", "Declining" and "Preservation Priority" as editorial labels to communicate the evidence and safeguarding context; they should not be read as a single official Government of India risk scale.

## Atlas map layer
District geometry is loaded from the public india-maps-data collection, which documents individual state TopoJSON files with district-level boundaries and attributes such as district name and state name. The repo describes these files as visualization-oriented geographic data and links direct CDN versions. See https://github.com/udit-001/india-maps-data
